Distance sampling estimates

The Estimates table summarises the camera-trap distance sampling (CTDS) density result for the selected species. Values come from the fitted detection function and dht2 density estimation in the R Distance package.

Metrics include:

  • Density (per km²): Estimated number of animals per square kilometre in the study area.
  • Standard error: Standard error of the density estimate.
  • Lower CI / Upper CI: Lower and upper bounds of the 95% confidence interval for density.
  • CV: Coefficient of variation of the density estimate (standard error divided by the estimate). Higher values indicate greater relative uncertainty.
  • Detections: Number of independent detections used after time-to-independence thinning.
  • Sites: Number of camera sites included in the analysis (including sites with effort but no detections of the selected species).
  • Sample fraction (FOV/360): Horizontal camera field of view divided by 360°. This is the fraction of a full circle covered by each camera.
  • Effective detection radius (m): Effective strip half-width / detection radius implied by the fitted detection function (metres).
  • Min / max truncation (m): Truncation distances applied when fitting the detection function. If max truncation was not set, the maximum observed detection distance is used.
  • Distance binning: Whether distance cutpoints were used when fitting the detection function.
  • Selected model: Name of the detection-function model chosen by automatic model selection.
  • Model family: Key-function family of the selected model (uniform, half-normal, or hazard-rate).
  • Selection method: How the final model was chosen (QAIC within each key-function family, then χ²/df comparison among family winners).
  • Overdispersion (χ²/df): Chi-square goodness-of-fit statistic divided by degrees of freedom for the selected model. Used in the final model comparison step.
  • Activity multiplier applied: Whether temporal availability was estimated and passed to dht2 as a multiplier (Howe et al. 2017).
  • Temporal availability rate / SE: Estimated proportion of camera operating time when animals were active and available for detection, and its standard error (shown when the activity multiplier is enabled).
  • Camera hours per day: Hours per 24 h day that cameras were recording, used to scale the activity rate.
  • Activity time scale: Clock uses wall-clock timestamps; Solar converts timestamps to solar time of day using mean trap-site coordinates and a UTC offset.

Reliable inference generally requires sufficient camera sites and independent detections. Very few sites often produce wide confidence intervals even when the point density looks plausible.
